Since 2013, the IEA in Paris has been located in an exceptional site that the City of Paris has dedicated to promoting the humanities and social sciences: the Hôtel de Lauzun. Built between 1656 and 1660 for Charles Gruyn, financier and secretary to the king, it is one of the most beautiful mansions in the capital. It features remarkable interior décor and has hosted many extraordinary figures over the years, including the Count of Lauzun, Théophile Gautier, and Charles Baudelaire.
During their stay in 1845, the salons of the Hôtel de Lauzun, then known as the Hôtel Pimodan, were regularly used for the very private parties of the Club des Haschichins, where artists and personalities of the time experimented with “artificial paradises.” [Learn more](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Lauzun)
